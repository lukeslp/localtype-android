LocalType experimental next-word model (cifg_int8.bin)

Luke Steuber trained this model on the corpus-v3 training split from the five sources below,
plus the Tatoeba English sentences in item 6 and the C4 web sentences in item 7. The corpus-v3 texts were filtered, normalized,
deduplicated and divided into training, development and test splits; the English vocabulary was
learned from that training split. The Tatoeba sentences went through the same line rules and an
exact dedupe against corpus-v3 and the scored evaluation sets, and lines naming Tatoeba's stock
characters were dropped. The C4 sentences were chosen by a filter built from the trainer's own
text messages; those messages only ranked public sentences, and none of their text is in the
training text. A CIFG-LSTM was then trained for 102,195 updates (about 2.4 passes),
selected on human-source development perplexity, and its weights were quantized to int8. NUS SMS,
Taskmaster-1 and OpenAssistant prompter turns were oversampled eight times; Taskmaster-3, SODA,
Tatoeba and C4 counted once. This file contains model weights and a vocabulary, rather than a copy of
the source corpus. The source authors have not endorsed LocalType. The sources are provided as-is
and without warranties under their respective licenses.

   Corpus", Language Resources and Evaluation 47(2), 2013.
   https://doi.org/10.25540/WVM0-4RNX
   Release mirror: kite1988/nus-sms-corpus, commit bf0a365170d324e61e4025edf363d400f1edd1a6.
   Only English messages were used, with the release's redaction tags removed. Human-written.
   License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0).
   https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

2. Taskmaster-1 written self-dialogs, TM-1-2019/self-dialogs.json.
   Byrne et al., "Taskmaster-1: Toward a Realistic and Diverse Dialog Dataset", EMNLP 2019.
   Copyright Google LLC.
   https://github.com/google-research-datasets/Taskmaster
   Commit d92cb6af3005f1dc09c39e75e7daf4a04905e00b.
   Only USER utterances were used; spoken woz-dialogs were excluded. Human-written.
   License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0).
   https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

3. OpenAssistant Conversations 2 (OpenAssistant/oasst2).
   Köpf et al., "OpenAssistant Conversations: Democratizing Large Language Model Alignment",
   NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks, arXiv:2304.07327.
   https://huggingface.co/datasets/OpenAssistant/oasst2
   Revision 179dd21fc55192153d94adb0e0ce8f69e222bf75, file
   2023-11-05_oasst2_ready.messages.jsonl.gz.
   Only English prompter turns that were not synthetic, deleted or rejected in review were used;
   assistant turns were excluded. Human-written.
   License: Apache License 2.0. A copy is included in Apache-2.0.txt alongside this notice.
   https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0

4. Taskmaster-3 (TicketTalk) self-dialogs, TM-3-2020/data/data_00.json through data_19.json.
   Byrne et al., "TicketTalk: Toward human-level performance with end-to-end, transaction-based

   https://github.com/google-research-datasets/Taskmaster/tree/master/TM-3-2020
   Commit d92cb6af3005f1dc09c39e75e7daf4a04905e00b.
   Only customer utterances (speaker user) were used; agent turns were excluded. Human-written.
   License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0).
   https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

5. SODA (allenai/soda).
   Kim et al., "SODA: Million-scale Dialogue Distillation with Social Commonsense

   https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/soda
   Revision fdc848ab0183208ea7808206c91c724414d0a071.
   Both speakers' dialogue turns were used. These dialogues were generated by GPT-3.5
   (InstructGPT); they are synthetic and are not counted as human-written.
   License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0).
   https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

6. Tatoeba English sentences (https://tatoeba.org).
   Tatoeba is a collaborative collection of example sentences maintained by Association Tatoeba.
   English export eng_sentences.tsv.bz2, downloaded 2026-11-14 02:59 UTC, SHA-256
   1a2d75ac07aa5d035bff4e2bf053139dfd3b1f1645f6fd3d76fc852cc9ef6020.

7. C4, English (allenai/c4, "en"): sentences from two of its 1,024 training files.
   C4 is the Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus, web pages from Common Crawl's April 2019 crawl,
   cleaned as described in Raffel et al., "Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a
   Unified Text-to-Text Transformer", JMLR 21(140), 2020, and prepared for download by the
   Allen Institute for AI.
   https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/c4
   Files en/c4-train.00000-of-01024.json.gz, SHA-256
   8ef8d75b0e045dec4aa5123a671b4564466b0707086a7ed1ba8721626dfffbc9, and
   en/c4-train.00001-of-01024.json.gz, SHA-256
   b945059cd1a343cabe311881b7840a6f0363f570e745a0eff0e687e266f6b55d, fetched 2026-11-13.
   1,000,000 sentences of 3 to 25 words were used, chosen from 10,847,777 after the same line
   rules and an exact dedupe against the other sources and the scored evaluation sets. The
   sentences come from public web pages whose authors the dataset does not name; how each page
   was written is not recorded.

   The underlying pages are also subject to the Common Crawl terms of use.
   https://commoncrawl.org/terms-of-use

Vocabulary selection also used LocalType's words_en.txt, a word-frequency list derived from
hermitdave/FrequencyWords (OpenSubtitles 2018 English), https://github.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords,
licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International. It shaped which words
are in the vocabulary; it is not training text. Its own notice is wordlists-NOTICE.txt alongside
this one.

The vocabulary's emoji tokens come from LocalType's emoji_en.txt, a word-to-emoji list
written for LocalType. The separate emoji panel uses Unicode and CLDR data; its attribution
and license terms are in Unicode-LICENSE.txt.
